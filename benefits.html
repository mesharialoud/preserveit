Benefits

What it's for — and how it pays off over time

Pumping is hours of work. Freeze-drying protects that work so it can keep feeding your baby long after your freezer would have run out of room — or power.

Book a pickupSee offerings

Who it's for

The mother with a full freezer

Turn a freezer stash into a single shelf and stop rotating bags.

The mother going back to work

Send pre-portioned sachets to nursery or the nanny — no thawing schedule.

The travelling family

Summer abroad or Umrah: carry weeks of milk in hand luggage, no ice packs.

The mother of a high-lipase baby

Powder that many babies accept better than thawed high-lipase milk.

Your milk across the first two years

0–6 m

Build a calm reserve

Freeze extra milk as normal. Send it for freeze-drying before it gets too old in the freezer.

6–12 m

Mix into first foods

With your doctor's go-ahead, stir powder into purées and cereals — familiar taste, extra nourishment.

Back to work

Bottles without the stress

Sachets mean exact portions at nursery or with a caregiver.

12–24 m

Keep going after weaning

A reserve of your own milk is available for the months after you've stopped pumping.

The long-run value

Nothing wasted

Milk that would have expired in the freezer — or been lost in a power cut — stays usable.

Peace of mind

No freezer-failure worry in Kuwait's summer heat.

Space back

Months of milk in a shoebox-sized space.

Your milk, your baby

Returned only to you, tracked by batch, never shared.

The honest part

Freeze-drying doesn't make milk better, and it isn't sterile. It's a storage method. For premature or newborn babies, or any medical concern, talk to your paediatrician first.
